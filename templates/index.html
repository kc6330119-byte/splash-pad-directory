Frequently Asked Questions

Everything families need to know about splash pads

A splash pad (also called a spray pad or spray park) is a recreational water play area featuring ground-level water jets, spray nozzles, dumping buckets, and other interactive water features. Unlike pools or water parks, splash pads have no standing water — making them a safe, zero-depth option for young children and toddlers. Most are free to use and located in public parks.
Many splash pads are completely free, especially those operated by city parks and recreation departments. Some privately operated splash pads or those inside water parks charge an admission fee. Use our Free Admission filter to find splash pads near you with no cost to enter.
Most outdoor splash pads in the US open between Memorial Day (late May) and Labor Day (early September), though this varies by region. Warmer states like Florida, Texas, and Arizona may open earlier or run year-round. Cooler northern states typically have shorter seasons. Always check the specific listing for seasonal dates before visiting, as hours and opening dates vary by location.
Yes — splash pads are one of the safest water play options for toddlers and young children because there is no standing water to fall into or drown in. The ground-level water features are low-pressure and designed for kids of all ages. That said, adult supervision is always required. Swim diapers are recommended for infants. Browse our Best for Toddlers listings for the most toddler-friendly options.
The terms are often used interchangeably. A splash pad typically refers to a smaller, ground-level water play area — often a flat pad with spray jets embedded in the surface. A spray park usually refers to a larger dedicated facility with multiple water structures, spray zones, and features for a wider age range. Both are zero-depth (no standing water) and suitable for children.
Pack swimwear or clothes that can get wet, water shoes (splash pad surfaces can get hot in direct sun), sunscreen, towels, a change of clothes, and plenty of water to drink. For toddlers, bring swim diapers. If the splash pad has picnic areas, pack a lunch or snacks. Check our listings for amenities like shade, restrooms, and snack bars so you know what's available on-site before you go.
